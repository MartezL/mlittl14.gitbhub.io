How to Buy a JDM Vehicle

Buying a Japanese Domestic Market vehicle can be exciting, but there are several steps between finding the car and putting it on the road. Use this guide as a simple overview of the process.

1

Choose the JDM Vehicle

Decide what vehicle you want and establish a realistic budget. Compare vehicles from Japanese dealers, exporters, and auctions before making a purchase.

Watch for: Purchase price is only part of the total cost. Leave room in your budget for transportation, shipping, taxes, fees, and registration.

2

Check Import Eligibility

Before paying for the vehicle, determine whether it can legally be imported into the destination country. Import rules can depend on the vehicle's age, specifications, and destination.

Watch for: A vehicle that is legal to own or drive in Japan is not automatically legal to import and register in another country.

3

Inspect the Vehicle

Review the vehicle's condition before committing to the purchase. Check available inspection information, maintenance history, mileage, accident history, rust, modifications, and auction documentation.

Watch for: Photos alone may not show mechanical problems, corrosion, or previous repairs. You can hire an independent pre-purchase inspection service.

4

Purchase & Collect Documents

Complete the purchase through the seller or exporter and keep the documents associated with the vehicle and sale. These records are important when arranging shipment and completing the import process.

Keep organized: Save copies of invoices, ownership/export paperwork, and shipping documents.

5

Arrange Shipping

Select a shipping method and arrange transportation from the vehicle's location in Japan to the departure port and then to the destination country. Confirm what is included in the shipping quote.

Watch for: Port fees, inland transportation, insurance, and other charges may be separate from the advertised vehicle price.

6

Complete Customs & Import Requirements

When the vehicle arrives, complete the required customs and import procedures for the destination. Be prepared to provide the vehicle and shipping documents and pay applicable duties, taxes, or processing fees.

Important: Requirements vary by country, so verify the current rules with the appropriate government agencies before shipping.

7

Register & Prepare the Vehicle

After import clearance, complete any required inspections, registration, insurance, or local compliance steps before driving the vehicle on public roads.

Final check: Do not assume customs clearance automatically means the vehicle is ready for road use.
